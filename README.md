# THE INTAKE

*A test of your intake craftsmanship, but then funnier.*

A browser game for learning professionals. You sit down at your cubicle, a stakeholder asks for a course, and you get one call to find the real need. Then you write the need, a measurement plan and a one-page concept, defend it if it's bold, and get graded. An experiment by Yannick, learning strategist.

Design doc: https://claude.ai/code/artifact/1f84b894-1c45-429e-8651-b48ed630dd90

## Run it

It's a static site with no build step. Serve the folder over http (the scenarios are fetched, so `file://` won't work):

```
python3 -m http.server 8000
```

Open http://localhost:8000 and add a Gemini API key under "Gemini key". A player's own key stays in their browser and is sent only to Google. The default model is `gemini-3.5-flash-lite`; a remembered model from the retired 1.x or 2.x families falls back to it.

Without a key, the game uses the backend in `api/` when `js/config.js` points to it: Yannick's key, a few free weeks per player, then an unlock code or the player's own key.

Every window has a desktop icon (on phones, use the start menu). A player's first week opens with two **starter cases** (cards with `"tier": 0`: an open stakeholder, one clear cause, 10 questions and extra patience) and opens the Center of Excellence folder on the flowchart; later weeks leave the starters out. Every finished request is kept in **My scores.txt** on the desktop (in this browser, key `intake-scores`).

Every case beyond the starters has **touchpoints**: 10 questions for the whole request. The first call takes at most 5. After it, the **Follow-up** window offers data requests (1 question each, answered from the card's `data_doc` text, no Gemini; you first write one line on what you expect the data to show, and the document shows it next to what came back), up to 2 questions to the card's `frontline` person at the coffee machine, and one **callback** (up to 3 questions, fresh patience) once you have found something. Each hidden fact's `found_at` decides where it can turn up; the stakeholder only knows the facts tagged `call`. The scorecard says where a missed fact could have been found. Starter cases keep a single 10-question call.

A **week** is three or four full cases from Monday to Thursday, plus the board room on Friday, and its shape changes from week to week. Week 1: two starters and a tier-1 case. Week 2: two tier-1 cases and a tier-2 case. From week 3 the game rotates through three shapes with tier-2 and tier-3 cases. Unplayed cards come first.

**The story** (`js/story.js`) is set at Synergix e-Learning Solutions, a small learning agency, starting on Monday 3 September 2007. A cold open plays once before your first week, and again from the title screen: "AI that can write a whole course is fifteen years away. You still have time…". Each week Darren, the CEO, sends a mail. In weeks 1 to 4 you are on probation, with a rival called Global Multimedia Solutions, and week 4 is the Halvorsen tender. Real headlines from that week in 2007 turn up in the inbox from the day they happened, so the game calendar moves with you. On Friday, Darren's memo reacts to the board. You are **promoted to team lead** after a week of 64+ with every diagnosis at 15+, or automatically in week 4. The promotion is stored in `intake-scores` and is the hook for the team. Hard mode skips the story.

**The team** (`js/team.js`). After the promotion, every normal week is a team week: two requests of your own (Monday and Thursday) and three drafts from Bart (weak on risks), Priya (measures) and Jens (concepts). Each draft is built in code from a card nobody plays that week: four sound lines from the card's gold answer and one flaw from its traps, usually the junior's weak spot and about a third of the time somewhere else. You click the flawed line (4 points) and write a coaching note, which Gemini scores 0 to 3 (doubled to 6) and answers in the junior's voice. The team score is shown on the end screen and in My scores; it does not change your title. To try it without playing four weeks, open the game with `?promote=1` (and `?promote=0` to undo).

**My scores** opens with two charts: every request over time, and your score parts over the last five requests as a share of their maximum, with the weakest one called out and a tip.

**The 2000s layer** (`js/retro.js`). All art is drawn in SVG and CSS, and every name is made up. Mieke's window is a Doors Messenger chat ("Mieke says:"), with a Nudge button and pop-ups in the corner when someone pings you at the start of a request. The brick phone (the 📱 in the taskbar tray, the start menu, or "Play Snake while you wait") sits on the desk as an object, not a window: drag it around, put it away with ✕ or its red key. It has texts from people outside the office and Snake, with the high score kept in `intake-snake`. Staply, a stapler with eyes, offers confidently bad advice at most once per request on the case board, the one-pager or the board slide. He can be switched off from the start menu (`intake-staply`) and never appears in hard mode. Wallpapers (start menu, Display properties) unlock with the best title you have finished a week with: Green Hill, then Azure Swoosh, Aquarium, Autumn Road and Night Skyline. The choice is kept in `intake-wallpaper`.

**Doors Aero.** The office PC runs Doors Classic, the blue-and-beige look every office still had in 2007. The promotion to team lead comes with the new PC: glass window frames, a dark glass taskbar, a round start orb, glossy buttons and the Aero Aurora wallpaper (the Frutiger Aero look of the time). IT explains it in a mail that week, and Display properties switches back to Classic if you prefer. All of it is `body.aero` in `styles.css`; `Retro.setAero` in `js/retro.js` handles the unlock.

**CoE Academy** (`js/academy.js`): each normal week assigns one of six modules, which waits on top of the desk on Monday morning (in the very first week it waits until the second request, so the game starts with a request; from week 2 the Monday stand-up comes first and the module waits on the desk icon): *Ask before you make*, *Is training the answer?*, *How will you know?*, *Who can say no*, *Read the proposal like a reviewer* and *One slide for the board*. Each module has four multiple-choice questions with instant feedback, then three things to look out for in your cases. The tips are not pinned to the case files: remembering them is part of the practice. Modules are scored in code with no Gemini call. They never count toward the week's score, but My scores.txt keeps the result. Hard mode has no module.

**The morning call** (`js/morning.js`, "Today's call" on the title screen): the small daily version, about three minutes, Monday to Friday. Everyone gets the same stakeholder on the same day: the card is picked by a seeded shuffle of the deck from the call number, which counts working days from Monday 12 October 2026, and the game date is a 2007 weekday from 3 September on. You get three free-text questions (one Gemini call each, tagged good, ok or weak like on the real call), then make the lever call and write the real problem in one sentence. The reveal is checked in code from the card: the lever call, the real need next to what you wrote, the facts the call could have found and the question that opens it up. The share line says how you played, never the answer, so a colleague can play the same call. "Take the full case" plays that stakeholder as a one-case run. There are no streaks or points: the screen says how many calls you took this month, and `intake-scores` keeps them under `mornings`. Coming back the same day shows your reveal again (`intake-morning`); at the weekend the office is closed. On the backend a morning call is its own small run (`calls_per_morning`, the same run all day for one browser, at most `mornings_per_ip` a day per IP) and never uses up a free week. Test it on another date with `?morning=2026-10-14`.

**Try one case** (title screen): a single tier-1 request in about ten minutes, for a first look or a lunch break. 7 questions (4 on the first call, 1 at the coffee machine, a callback of 2), the case board or memo that fits your experience, the full scorecard, then done: no stand-up, CoE module, team or board room. It lands in My scores as "One case", stand-ups can bring it back later, and it does not count as a week, a title for wallpapers, or toward the promotion.

**Take it to Monday:** every scorecard ends with one if-then plan for a real request: "When someone asks me for ___, I will first ask ___", pre-filled with your best-rated question from the call. Kept plans (the last 12) live in My scores and print on the **pocket card** in the Center of Excellence folder: the folder on one page plus your plans, which you can save as a plain HTML file for your computer or phone. No Gemini call, no points.

**Measurement drill** (`js/drill.js`, 🎯 on the desktop and in the Start menu, and a link under Measurement on any scorecard that is not a 4): one case, measurement only, in about three minutes. Mieke gives you the real need, then you sort the card's own measures into "shows it worked" (its baseline, behaviour and business metric), "looks good, proves nothing" (its measurement traps) and "they don't have it" (data the stakeholder lacks), and see why each one sits where it does. Then you write the rule that stops or scales the pilot and check it against the four things it needs (a limited first step, a behaviour or business measure, the result that makes you stop, and when); the ticks start as a guess from your wording and you correct them. You compare with the card's measures and say whether your rule would have told the client to stop or scale. From the desktop it picks a case you played with your weakest measurement score, else any non-starter case. No Gemini call and no points; My scores.txt keeps the count and your sorting over the last five.

**Monday stand-up** (`js/standup.js`): from week 2, Monday starts with Mieke reading you up to two requests from earlier weeks as if they just landed again. From memory you write the first question you would ask and make the lever call (yes, partly, no). She then shows the question that opened the case up, what was really going on, and whether your lever call was right (checked in code). You say whether your question went after the same thing; both right is "got it", one is "partly", neither is "missed". A case retires after two "got it" at least six days apart, a miss resets it, and nothing comes back within a day of its last review. Never-got cases come first, then the lowest score. There is no Gemini call and no points; My scores.txt keeps the recall count. Hard mode has no stand-up. `node tests/standup.cjs` checks the spacing.

**The help fades**: in week 1 the case board has every chip, and Mieke shows her own diagnosis once you lock yours in. In week 2 only the diagnosis keeps its chips; measure and concept are your own one-liners. From week 3 you write a blank one-pager under four headings (the board is still one click away).

**Friday: the board room.** After the last request you take one concept from the week to the board (CFO, CHRO with the privacy officer, COO) on one slide with six boxes: problem, evidence, concept, measure, risks, and the ask. You build it in Pitchwright 2007 (`js/slides.js`), on a 4:3 slide that starts from your write-up (blank in hard mode), with a design, a transition (star wipe included), WordArt and clip art. Then you present it full screen, one box per click, while the board reacts in the front row: a nod, a note, a frown, a raised eyebrow, a yawn or a calculator. Style never changes the score, but a flashy slide earns a whispered remark. Each member nods if the slide already answers their concern, or asks you one question to answer live. The chair grades the slide (40 points) and each member (20; a nod is a full 20) and decides: approved, approved as a pilot, or rejected. The board score counts as much as the week's requests for your title, and it goes on the share card and in My scores.txt.

**Hard mode** (a button on the title screen): three tier-2 and tier-3 cases, 6 questions each (4 on the first call), no case file, no Mieke, and one memo of at most 150 words instead of the board. The week ends with a **share card**: your title, the hidden facts you found, how the defence rounds went and your best line, as a picture (1200 x 627) and as text. Only a hard-mode week gets the HARD MODE badge and the LinkedIn button.

**Test mode:** add `?debug` to the address once (`?debug=0` turns it off again). An **Autofill** button appears in the tray: in the inbox or on the call it plays a scripted call that surfaces every hidden fact (no Gemini calls), on the case board or in memo mode it fills every step from the card's model answer, and in the defence round it fills the answers. The reaction and the examiner are still real Gemini calls.

After the call you write up on the **case board**: five short steps (diagnose with evidence from the call and cause tags, is learning the lever, a four-rung measurement ladder, the concept, the top two blockers), each locked before the next, with Mieke's feedback after the diagnosis, the lever and the risks. The steps become a one-page memo for the stakeholder. Memo mode keeps the original long form.

Scoring per request, out of 100, the same way on the case board, in a memo and in hard mode (`js/scoring.js`): need diagnosis 25 (the examiner's read of the problem 13, the weighted hidden facts you surfaced 8, and the lever 4: right, one step off for a quarter, or wrong; on the case board it is your pick, in a memo the examiner reads your call from the text), measurement 20, concept fit 20, right-sized ambition 15 (scaled by fit, so a concept counts as right-sized only as far as it fits the need; half the examiner's read of whether it is the smallest thing that would work, half how well the concept's boldness fits the client; originality is not scored), risks and dependencies 10, pitch and defence 10. Facts from the call and the coffee machine count when you hear them. A fact from a data document counts only once you use it: cite the document on the case board (diagnosis evidence or a measurement rung), or build the write-up on it, which the examiner reports as `factsUsed` (a majority of its three runs). The scorecard lists a document you had but did not use as "You had this in ... but did not use it". Hidden facts count once: the examiner is told to leave them and the lever out of its diagnosis score. Concept fit asks who owns each cause: a 4 routes every cause that is not about skill to the person who owns it and scopes the learning part honestly. On the case board you name an owner per cause (us, the client, or someone you name) and Mieke flags any non-skill cause you put on the learning team's plate. The case board's cause tags, cited evidence and blocker picks are shown on the scorecard as practice checks without points, so a week-3 memo and a week-1 board are on one scale.

**Mieke shows you round** (`js/tour.js`). On your very first morning, right after you log on, Mieke calls from two cubicles over ("Answer", or "Not now, I will find my way"), then shows you round in speech bubbles with a spotlight: welcome, Doors Mail, herself and the start menu, then your request. The rest of the desk she explains in one bubble each, the first time you need it (the call's meters, the case board, Follow-up, My scores); those hints do not block anything and are kept in `intake-told-hint-*`. Arrow keys step through the tour and Escape ends it. It runs once (`intake-told-tour`), her first-morning message lands in her chat afterwards, and "Tour with Mieke" in the start menu replays it without the call.

**The desk grows with you.** Day 1 shows six icons (Center of Excellence, Doors Mail, Case file, My notes, Phone, Mieke). The case board turns up after your first call, Follow-up with your first follow-up, the CoE Academy when its module is due, and My scores, the Measurement drill and the Recycle Bin after your first scorecard (`intake-desk`). Everything is in the start menu from the start, and players with three or more requests behind them see the whole desk.

**Week 1 runs slower.** Tuesday is only request 2: the first CoE module waits until Thursday morning, before request 3. Texts from outside the office arrive without a ring in week 1, and Staply waits until the last request of the week. When a window opens by itself (the module, the stand-up), Mieke's morning line goes quietly into her chat instead of popping up with a ding. Between two days a short card says you went home and came back (click to skip). The memo-mode link is hidden on the very first case board, and the Gemini model sits under "Advanced" on the key screen.

**The office** (`js/office.js`, pictures in `media/office/`). The Synergix floor is a scene built and rendered in Blender (Cycles), not real-time 3D, so it looks the same on every laptop, including locked-down corporate ones without WebGL. On your very first morning a 12-second film walks you out of the lift, past reception and Mieke's desk, into your chair and onto your monitor, which goes black and becomes the boot screen. It has three captions and a Skip button; it is skipped by itself when the system asks for reduced motion, when the browser saves data, or when the video has not started within four seconds (`intake-told-walkin`). Between requests you stand up and see the floor from above, like a diorama: places light up under the mouse, and each says one thing without a Gemini call. Your desk starts the next request; Mieke has a line on your last scorecard; Darren's door has his mail of the week taped to it; the canteen TV shows that day's 2007 headline; the coffee machine is still broken; after the last request the board room takes you to Friday. The same places are a list of buttons under the picture, for phones and keyboards. Hard mode and "Try one case" skip the floor. The scene's source is in `tools/office/` (`scene.py` builds the floor in Blender's Python API, `textures.py` paints the screens and posters, `post.py` makes the hit mask, hover glows and `js/office-map.js`, `encode.sh` grades and encodes the film); see the README there to render it again.

**The first day is quiet on purpose.** Clock in without a key and the key screen says the game needs one, links to Google AI Studio, and "Save and clock in" goes straight on. Your very first request opens the inbox alone, with only the request unread: the case file is its 📎 attachment (with a Call button of its own) and the notes come with the call. Mieke's one ping is the only guide; there is no Staply on that first request, and the CoE Academy mail only arrives when the module is due. Rules are explained the moment they matter instead of up front: the first time a question costs patience, the call says why (once, kept in `intake-told-patience`), the meters have tooltips, and the case board and the bet say what is missing next to the button, taking you to the owners row if that is it. A new day says good morning unless the module is already waiting with its own hello, and "Back to work" puts the request in front of you.

**The scorecard teaches.** It opens with one line to work on next: the part with the most points left and its next level. Under each part the examiner says in one sentence what would have lifted it one level in this case; why it scored what it did, the practice checks and a fold-out with the card's model answer (the need and the lever, the measurement plan, a solid concept, a bolder version that still fits, and the people who can block it) sit under "Why this score". Under "The question you didn't ask", **Ask it now** lets you put one question to the stakeholder with hindsight and shows which fact it would have opened. It scores nothing and costs one Gemini call.

**The examiner runs three times** on every request, side by side, and the scorecard uses the median of each dimension (with that run's quote), the most common defence outcome and lever call, and the comments of the run closest to the median. If one or two runs fail, the rest still count. The game keeps the last 12 graded write-ups, exactly as the examiner saw them, in `intake-graded`.

**The bet:** before the stakeholder reads your concept, you say how bold it is (0 to 3) and how much risk this client can take (0 to 3); a concept at 2 or more needs a way back. The stakeholder rates the concept's boldness three times without being told its appetite number, and the median counts. A concept as bold as the client's appetite, or one step bolder with a concrete way back, is right-sized. The examiner grades the way back: concrete means a limited first step and the result on behaviour or a business number that makes you stop; "we stop it", a date alone or a satisfaction threshold is vague and does not count; too timid or too far costs ambition points. Guessing the client's appetite right earns +2, calling your own concept right +1. Players can find the appetite by asking: the stakeholder describes it in words, never as a number. A defence round triggers when the concept is bolder than the appetite: holding your ground with reasons, or adapting the plan to a fair objection, earns +3; a weak defence halves ambition, backing down without a reason zeroes it.

## Deploying

**GitHub Pages:** pushing to `main` deploys via `.github/workflows/pages.yml` (set Pages source to GitHub Actions). The `api/` folder is not published there.

**yannickwillems.be (shared hosting with PHP and MySQL):**

1. In your hosting panel, create a MySQL database and user. Run `api/schema.sql` once (phpMyAdmin, Import). A database made before the morning call also needs `api/upgrade-2.sql`, once.
2. Copy `api/config.sample.php` to `intake-config.php` **one folder above your web root** (next to `public_html`, not inside it) and fill in the Gemini key, database details, a long random `ip_salt`, and the free runs per player. If your host won't let you write there, save it as `api/config.php` instead; the `.htaccess` in `api/` blocks downloads of it.
3. Upload `index.html`, `styles.css`, `js/`, `scenarios/`, `media/` and `api/` over FTP to a subfolder, for example `public_html/the-intake/`.
4. In the uploaded `js/config.js`, set `api: 'api/index.php'` and, if you like, `donateUrl` to your donation page.
5. Open the game. The title screen should say how many free weeks are left.

To let the GitHub Pages version use the same backend, set `api` in `js/config.js` to the full URL (`https://yannickwillems.be/the-intake/api/index.php`); `https://recklessnative.github.io` is already in `allowed_origins`.

**Unlock codes:** edit and run `api/new-code.sql`. Each code grants a number of extra runs and can be redeemed by a set number of players. **Safety limits** in the config: `calls_per_run` stops a runaway run, `calls_per_morning` and `mornings_per_ip` do the same for the morning call, and `daily_calls` closes the game for the day before the bill can surprise you.

The backend stores a salted hash of each IP address (never the IP itself), a random browser token, and run counts. A week counts against the browser token only once it was played (its first scorecard, or `charge_after_calls` Gemini calls); starting a week again before that hands back the same week. Every week started also counts against the IP, which may start three times as many, so colleagues behind one office IP can still play.

The game saves the week in the browser after every step, so players can close the tab and resume from the title screen.

## Test

- `node tests/smoke.cjs` plays a full three-request week on an own key, in headless Chromium with Gemini mocked (needs Playwright). `node tests/smoke.cjs . /tmp --mobile` does the same at phone width.
- `bash tests/backend.sh` checks the backend against SQLite and a mock Gemini: cap, unlock codes, call limits, CORS (needs PHP with pdo_sqlite).
- `bash tests/backend-e2e.sh` plays the game through the backend: claim a run, play, hit the cap, unlock.
- `node tests/scoring.cjs` checks the scoring rules: one measure across modes, facts counted once, the median merge.
- `GEMINI_KEY=... node tests/judge-spread.cjs` grades the same write-ups five times with the real examiner and reports how far scores move, as single runs and as the median of three. By default it uses three made-up write-ups per card (strong, passable and weak, built from the card) and flags cards where they come out of order. `--saved file.json` adds write-ups saved by the game (in the browser console, `copy(localStorage['intake-graded'])`, then save it to a file); `--cards`, `--runs` and `--out` narrow or keep the results. It costs real calls: 23 cards × 3 write-ups × 5 runs is 345. `--calibration` grades the 30 committee write-ups in `tests/calibration/` instead and reports how often the examiner lands on the committee's consensus, per dimension (see that folder's README).
